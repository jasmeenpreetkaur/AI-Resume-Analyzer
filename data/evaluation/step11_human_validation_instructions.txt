AI RESUME ANALYZER
STEP 11 - HUMAN VALIDATION INSTRUCTIONS
========================================

PURPOSE
-------
This file evaluates whether the Step 10 hybrid matching score
corresponds to human judgments of resume-job relevance.

IMPORTANT
---------
Do NOT try to reproduce the Step 10 score.

Do NOT use Step 9 evidence_score.

Do NOT assume that a high model score means the candidate should
be hired.

The task is ONLY:

    "How relevant is this resume to this specific job?"

Use the resume and the complete job information provided.

RELEVANCE LABELS
----------------

0 = NOT RELEVANT
    The resume is clearly unrelated to the job.

1 = WEAK MATCH
    Some transferable or overlapping information exists, but the
    candidate appears poorly aligned with the main requirements.

2 = MODERATE MATCH
    The resume has meaningful overlap with the job, but important
    requirements or qualifications are missing.

3 = STRONG MATCH
    The resume matches most major requirements and responsibilities.

4 = VERY STRONG MATCH
    The resume shows strong alignment across the major skills,
    experience, responsibilities and/or qualifications.

IMPORTANT
---------
Do not judge personality, appearance, prestige, age, gender,
ethnicity, religion or other protected/personal characteristics.

Evaluate job-related relevance only.

HUMAN CONFIDENCE
----------------

1 = Low confidence
2 = Moderate confidence
3 = High confidence

HUMAN NOTES
-----------
Briefly explain the main reason for your rating.

Example:

"Strong Python and machine-learning experience, but limited
evidence of production deployment."

SECOND EVALUATOR
----------------
If a second evaluator is available, they should independently
annotate the same samples without seeing evaluator 1's labels.

This allows inter-rater agreement to be calculated.

DO NOT CHANGE
-------------
Do not modify:
- resume_id
- job_id
- score_stratum
- step10_hybrid_match_score
- resume_text
- job_title
- job_description
- validation_sample_id

Only fill the human annotation columns.
